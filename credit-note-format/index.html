GST credit note format: what it must show

A credit note is how a registered supplier reduces an invoice already issued. Under GST you do not tear up or edit the old invoice: you issue a numbered credit note that points to it.

When a GST credit note is issued

Section 34 of the CGST Act lets the supplier issue a credit note when, after a tax invoice:

  • the taxable value or the tax charged on the invoice turns out to be more than it should be,
  • the buyer returns goods, or
  • goods or services supplied are found deficient (short, damaged, not as ordered).

Only the supplier issues a GST credit note. If a buyer sends you a "credit note" for goods returned to you, it is not a GST credit note; ask the supplier to issue one, or treat the buyer's document as a commercial note only.

What it must show

The GST rules list the details. A credit note should have:

  1. Your name, address and GSTIN.
  2. The words Credit Note, so it is clear what kind of document it is.
  3. A serial number, unique for the financial year, up to 16 characters (letters, numbers, - and /), for example CN/26-27/001.
  4. The date of issue.
  5. The recipient's name, address and GSTIN. If they are not registered, their name, address and the state name and code.
  6. The number and date of the original tax invoice (one note can cover more than one invoice; list them).
  7. The value of goods or services, the tax rate, and the amount of tax being credited.
  8. Signature of you or someone you authorise.

Common mistakes

  • Editing or cancelling an invoice after it went into your GSTR-1, instead of issuing a credit note.
  • Using the invoice number series for credit notes. Keep a separate series.
  • Not quoting the original invoice, or quoting a different rate of tax from it.
  • Crediting the whole invoice when only part was returned. Enter only the difference.
  • Missing the time limit: see time limit for credit notes.
