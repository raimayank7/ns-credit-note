Credit note vs debit note under GST

Both correct an invoice already issued. A credit note brings the bill down; a debit note takes it up. Under GST, both are issued by the supplier.

The difference at a glance

Credit noteDebit note
Issued bySupplierSupplier
WhenInvoice value or tax was too high; goods returned; supply deficientInvoice value or tax was too low
Supplier's GSTGoes downGoes up
Buyer's input tax creditGoes down (buyer reduces it)Goes up (buyer may claim more)
Number series hereCN/26-27/001DN/26-27/001
Time limitYes (see below)No limit to issue; buyer's credit has a limit

Examples

  • Goods returned: credit note for the value and tax of the returned goods.
  • Rate billed wrongly high: billed 100 units at ₹520 instead of ₹500. Credit note: 100 × ₹20 = ₹2,000 + GST.
  • Rate billed wrongly low: billed ₹480 instead of ₹500 on 100 units. Debit note: ₹2,000 + IGST 18% ₹360 = ₹2,360.
  • Wrong GST rate: charged 5% instead of 18% on ₹50,000. Debit note for the extra 13% tax, ₹6,500. Choose the reason "Tax corrected".
  • Extra freight agreed later: debit note for the freight and its GST.

Discount after the sale

A discount given after the sale (for example a year-end target discount) reduces the GST only when the conditions in the GST law are met: the discount must be linked to the specific invoices, it must be given through a GST credit note, and the buyer must reduce their input tax credit by the GST on it. The government has also asked suppliers to keep proof that the buyer did so. These conditions have been changed more than once, so check the current position with your accountant.

When the conditions are not met, you can still give the discount through a commercial (financial) credit note: it lowers the amount the customer owes but not your GST. In the maker, untick "Show GST on this document" for such a note and write "Commercial credit note, GST not reduced" in Notes.

How to make each one here

  1. Choose Credit note or Debit note in the form. The number series changes with it.
  2. Type the original invoice number and date, and pick the reason.
  3. Enter only the lines that change: the returned quantity, or the rate difference with quantity, at the original GST rate.
  4. Save, print both copies, and give the original to the customer.

What about a buyer's debit note?

Many buyers send a "debit note" to the supplier when they return goods. In accounts this is fine, but it is not a GST document. For the GST to be reduced, the supplier must issue the credit note. You can make either on this site; only the supplier's credit note counts for GST.

Not sure which to choose? Ask: is the customer paying less (credit note) or more (debit note) than the original invoice said?
